it closed old ones**: 5 findings, then 6, now 8. The validators were green after each pass, and are
green now. That is the important signal, and it is not a diligence signal.

Every one of these defect classes lives in **hand-maintained prose that asserts a fact no validator

### R3-F1 (trace failure policy) - high

> **Trace failure policy permits raw egress while QA requires fail-closed behavior**
> (`skills/measure-instrumentation-spec/references/TEMPLATE.md:110-160`)
> The egress row explicitly permits "send it raw", but the corresponding test unconditionally
> requires that no raw trace reach the collector. Storage has no failure-behavior decision row, yet
> its test assumes fail-closed behavior. The sentinel test also checks only the collector, so
> successful egress filtering followed by ineffective storage filtering can persist forbidden
> content without detection. This produces either an impossible specification or an unsafe
> fail-open implementation.
> Recommendation: Prohibit raw egress for disallowed data classes, add a separate storage-failure
> behavior row, and verify sentinel absence at both the collector and durable store during normal
> and forced-failure paths.

**Verification: CONFIRMED. Self-inflicted, in the round-2 fix.** The failure row offers "drop the
trace, block the request, or send it raw" while the negative test requires "no raw trace reaches the
collector". A PM choosing the third option writes a spec that cannot pass its own QA. The storage
side has a test but no corresponding decision row, which is the mirror-image gap of the one round 2
found on the egress side.

### R3-F2 (statistical overstatement) - high

> **The rule-of-three derivation and Orbit examples overstate statistical protection**
> (`skills/deliver-prd/references/TEMPLATE.md:143-163`)
> The 3/n rule is an approximate one-sided 95 percent upper confidence bound after zero
> independent, representative Bernoulli failures. The template states neither the confidence level
> nor the sampling assumptions and incorrectly calls 3/n the whole derivation. It also leaves the
> tolerable failure rate subjective, so the original PM-to-PM convergence problem remains. The Orbit
> examples compound this by claiming n=40 and n=80 while holding out only 25 percent. After tuning,
> only 10 and 20 untouched observations support a bound, giving exact 95 percent upper bounds near
> 25.9 percent and 13.9 percent, not 7.5 percent and 3.75 percent.
> Recommendation: Require preregistered p_max, confidence alpha, sampling frame, and independence
> assumptions. Derive n with ceil(log(alpha)/log(1-p_max)), define n as untouched final-evaluation
> cases, and label non-random curated slices as coverage judgments rather than population-rate
> bounds.

**Verification: CONFIRMED, and the arithmetic is exact.** Computed independently: for n=10 the
one-sided 95 percent bound is 1-0.05^(1/10) = 25.9 percent; for n=20 it is 13.9 percent. The round-2
fix derived its bound from the full slice size while the same template holds 25 percent back from
tuning, so the untouched sets are 10 and 20, not 40 and 80. The claimed protection is roughly 3.5x
better than what the design supports. The secondary charges also hold: "That is the whole
derivation" is glib and hides the independence and representativeness assumptions, and moving the
arbitrariness from N into the tolerable failure rate does not by itself make two PMs converge.

**This is the most serious finding across all three rounds.** It is a wrong number presented as
release evidence, introduced by a fix intended to stop exactly that.

## Convergence assessment and recommendation

**Not converged, and diverging.** 5, then 6, then 8. Continuing the same loop has no evidence behind
it: three consecutive fix passes each introduced fresh defects, and the two highs in this round were
both created by the pass that was meant to close the previous two highs.

R3-F2's recommended remedy asks for preregistered `p_max`, confidence `alpha`, a sampling frame,
independence assumptions, and `ceil(log(alpha)/log(1-p_max))` sizing inside a PM-facing template.
That is not a patch. It is novel methodology being authored under gate pressure, in a repo whose own
standing rule is that a framework gets tested against a no-framework control arm before it is built,
precisely so its author does not grade their own homework. The AI opportunity-shaping work was
killed on that test in August. The evaluation-sizing guidance is the same class of content and has
not had the same scrutiny.

**The recommendation to the maintainer is therefore to stop patching and re-scope**, rather than run
a fourth round against the same surface. Options are recorded in the release plan under the round-3
disposition.
